# External demand (exploration)

**Status:** Exploration. **Not** a domain, **not** a process, **not** a product spec, **not** tokenomics.  
**Kernel:** Entity · Relation · Intent · Occurrence · Domain. **No ExternalDemand primitive.**  
**Does not modify:** other corpus files.  
**Depends on:** `economic-engine.md`, `value.md` (W/P/E/S), `principles.md` (especially P1, P6, P7, P11, P16, P17, P20, P22), `information.md`, `AUDIT.md` v2 M.

**Hard question:** Who **outside the ordinary player economy** would actually pay for something Dynasty **already contains** — and receive something other than “the next buyer”?

Be aggressively skeptical. **Do not invent demand because the thesis needs it.** If there is no convincing answer, say so.

This document **does not create** an ExternalDemand domain or process.

---

## 0. What this is not answering

Not designed here: token supply, staking, APY, markets, prices, settlement, contracts implementation, custody, sponsorship deal sheets, legal advertising, lottery, betting.

**Not RVI** (already locked in `value.md`): token appreciation; deposits as such; P2P transfers as such; fees on those transfers as such.

**Not an export:** W lots (ore, mills, routes) are **not** real-world commodities. Saying they are would be fiction.

## 1. Define “external”

**External demand** is money that enters because the payer receives a **use, experience, association, or right they want for itself**, and the payer is **not merely playing the internal W/P/E loop**.

**Not automatically external:**

| Actor | Why they might look “outside” | Usually actually |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary player | Pays nothing extra | Internal participant |
| Wealthy player | Brings a lot of S | **Still a player.** Buying scale, lots, Intel, `control` contests is **internal E** (power, P11) |
| New player from outside | “New capital” | **Player entry.** Depositing to play is **participation funding**, not an export sale |
| Spectator | Not issuing World Intents | May be **audience**; demand is entertainment/sponsorship, not W output |
| Sponsor | Brand, not a dynasty | **Candidate external** if they buy attention/association/events — P20 |
| Organization (in-world) | Sounds institutional | If it issues Intents, it is a **player-shaped actor** |
| Business | “We need a customer” | Only external if they buy a **product they use outside ordinary play** |
| Researcher | Wants data | Candidate **if** they buy information as a product — integrity risk (§6) |
| Service buyer | Wants haul/research done | If they are a player, **internal E**. If they never play, **freelance platform risk** (§7) |
| Capital provider | Funds sites/orgs | **Financing**, not demand, unless someone else buys the output (§9) |
| Collector | Wants unique objects | Social/collectible; often **speculative** unless no-resale still holds (§5, §11) |
| Protocol participant | Holds/settles | **Not demand.** Coordination is not a customer |

**The important cut:**

> Someone can enter Dynasty **with money** and still only be **participating in the internal economy**.

A whale buying every mill is **not** REAL VALUE IN. It is a large player.

**External** here means: **the reason for payment is not “I need this to play / trade / win inside Dynasty, and I expect another participant to take it off me.”**

Borderline cases (pay-to-play, cosmetic, spectator ticket) are **real money** and must be classified honestly — often **legitimate monetization**, not proof of a **crypto-economic export**.

## 2. What can Dynasty actually sell?

Start from **what exists**: a persistent-player, seasonal-world, Observe→Choose→Act game that produces **W transformations**, **P capability**, **Information**, and (undesigned) **E** rights. Audience and history **may** exist if people play.

For each candidate: what is received; why want it; **would they pay if resale were impossible?**

| Candidate | Buyer receives | Why want it | No resale? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Participation / world access** | Right to issue Intents, be in W | The game | **Yes** — that is the product |
| Premium / special events | Extra access or instance | More play, status of being there | **Often yes** if the experience is the point |
| Access (in-world `access`) | Ability to enter a Site/route | Play advantage | **Yes for players**; outsider with no Intent to use it → **no** |
| World experiences | Being there (raid, discovery, season) | Entertainment | **Yes** |
| Information / Intel / maps | Claims about W | Decision advantage **in play** | Players: **yes**. Outsiders: only if they **use** it outside play — **unspecified product** |
| Discoveries | Knowledge Occurrence + maybe story | Mastery, history | Knowledge: **yes**. Tradable “discovery NFT”: **often no** |
| Blueprints | Capability / option (P) | Better Intents | **Yes** if they play. **No** if they only flip |
| Digital goods (W lots as “items”) | Situated stuff / listed E | Use in W | **Yes for players.** **No** as real-world goods |
| Digital rights / title | E claim on use/operate/exclude | Power or rent | **Yes** if they will **use** the right. **No** if only exit liquidity |
| Services (haul, guard, operate) | Intents performed | Progress in W | **Player-to-player.** Outsider: see §7 |
| Infrastructure | Sites/`connects` | Capacity | Same as title/`control` — **internal** unless outsider operates without playing (doesn’t fit fantasy) |
| Sponsorship inventory | Association, named events, optional branded opportunities | Brand / audience | **Yes** (they are not reselling the ad; they consume attention) |
| Attention / audience | Eyes, talk, clips | Marketing | **Yes**, **if audience exists**. Chicken-and-egg |
| Events | Occasion, stakes, story | Play + spectacle | Players **yes**. Sponsors **yes**. Speculators betting **not designed; not RVI** |
| Strategic opportunities | Remainder, positions | Play | **Internal.** Selling “the future” as a security → speculation / P12 tension |
| Ownership/title | Legal-ish claim | Use, status, or flip | Use: **maybe**. Flip-only: **speculation** |
| Computation / research / datasets | Useful output **outside** the game | Real work | **Does not emerge from current play** (§8) |

**Fatal if ignored:** tradability does not create a buyer. Scarcity of a W number does not create an outsider.

## 3. Paying to participate

Examples: entry, premium access, world/season access, optional paid events.

**Classification:** **B, and sometimes A as accounting, not as thesis.**

- **A (external revenue)** in the ordinary sense: a **customer** pays the operator for a product (the game). That is how games live. It is **not circular** if they would pay with **no resale** of a ticket (they consume play).
- **B (player funding)** in the RVI sense: the payer **is** the participant. Money in is **the cost of playing**, not payment for **world output exported**.
- **Both** is allowed as a sentence and **must not** be used to smuggle P17.

**Does it satisfy REAL VALUE IN → REAL VALUE OUT?**

- **In:** yes, new money from people who want to play (if they pay the protocol/operator, not only each other).
- **Out:** refunds, salaries, servers, **or** later settlement of **play-unrelated** claims — **not** “production profit from ore.”
- **P17 test:** named inflow that is **not P2P recirculation** — **participation fees can be that inflow.** They still **do not** prove that **building/discovering/owning in W** is what the outsider paid for. They prove people **value the game**.

**Do not reject** this as “just monetization.” It is the **only currently honest, strong, no-resale, no-token** demand.

**Do not pretend** it is the **crypto-economic** thesis. P1 wants play whose **building/discovery/ownership/earnings can have real economic significance** — not merely “WoW but you can also deposit.” Participation fees exist without any world economy at all.

**P18 tension:** if the **only** inflow is tickets, players are **customers of a house**, not obviously **owners/participants in economic outcomes**. That can still be a great game. It is a **weaker** reading of P1/P18.

## 4. Sponsorship

P20: external parties may participate **in the world**, not only as client banners.

**What a sponsor can buy (candidates, not a catalog):** attention; named/branded **Events**; optional sponsored **opportunities** (remainder that is clearly optional, not a pay-to-win vein); competition/season framing; cosmetic or experiential presence at Sites; audience interaction around spectacles.

**Sponsor receives:** association, story, reachable audience, sometimes exclusive naming of an Event — **consumed attention**, not a resale asset. **No-resale: typically still pays.**

**Player receives:** spectacle, prizes **if designed**, optional branded content, possibly distorted incentives if the sponsored thing is the **best** play.

**Does it create value without distorting play?** Only if sponsored content is **optional**, **not** the only remainder (P12), **not** a mastery skip (P11), and **raid/brand-safety** is honest (`AUDIT.md`: promising unraidable brands while the fantasy is Rust-like is a lie — **O32** in engine close).

**Meaningful economic input?** **Yes, in principle**, at **audience scale**. Before that, it is a **pitch**, not demand. Sponsorship **scales with spectacle**, not with mills.

**Distinguish:**

| Sponsorship revenue | Productive W output |
| --- | --- |
| Pays for **attention / association** | Pays for **transformed World state** |
| Exists if people **watch or talk** | Exists if players **operate Sites** |
| Does not require ore to be a commodity | Does not require an audience |

Both can be real. **They are not the same loop.** Calling sponsor money “the world producing value” **conflates the show with the factory**.

**Integrity:** pay-to-place Events can become the **real** map. Then veterans and capital chase **sponsor spawn**, not remainder. Flag if it happens.

## 6. Information as external product

Information is first-class. That does **not** make it an **export**.

**Would an outsider pay with no resale?**

- **To play better:** they are a **player** (or a coach of players) — **internal**.
- **For a dataset about a fictional world’s mills:** **almost never**, unless they are writing about Dynasty (journalism, academic **game** study) — tiny, not an economy.
- **For “emergent market intelligence”** as if W were a real commodity market: **cool but useless** unless W maps to a real market — **it does not**.
- **Forecasts of the game meta:** same as esports coaching — **player services**, not RVI.

**Damage to the game:** selling omniscient or near-omniscient World dumps **kills P22**. Selling **true current** deposit maps to the highest bidder is **pay-to-see**, adjacent to pay-to-master. Stale/public lore dumps are safer and **worth less**.

**Fun with external buyers?** Only if what they buy is **already public-ish** (recaps, spectacle, delayed history) — then it is **media**, not Intel.

**Unpaid data workers:** if play is **instrumented to produce training data / predictions for sale**, the fantasy becomes **labor**. Flag. The current Intent loop does **not** naturally emit an external information product.

**Skeptical conclusion:** Information is a **superb internal E**. It is **not** a demonstrated external product. Forcing it is the fastest way to ruin fog.

## 7. Player-generated services (sold off-world)

Logistics, research, strategy, creative work, moderation, event org, discovery, intelligence — **for outsiders**.

**Inside the game**, these are **why P2P E exists** (`economic-engine.md`). That is **good**.

**Sold to people who do not play:** Dynasty becomes a **freelance board** with a map skin. Buyers do not care about remainder, `control`, or Rust/Civ. Players optimize for **tickets from Discord**, not Observe→Choose→Act.

**Unacceptable as the economic thesis** if the **dominant** paid activity is off-world labor. **Acceptable** as rare edges (a documentary crew paying a guide) — **not a flywheel**.

## 9. Capital providers

Could someone fund infrastructure, production, Sites, orgs, Events, “world development”?

**They receive**, in honest categories:

- **Productive financing:** a claim on **output someone else wants to buy**. Today that someone is **other players** (circular relative to RVI) or **sponsors** (then capital is **funding a show**, still not W-as-commodity).
- **Ownership / `control` / title:** **power**. P11/P12: money must not own the unhappened, must not buy mastery. Funding a mill in exchange for `control` is **still internal**.
- **Revenue rights:** a cut of **fees or tickets** — then they are **investing in the operator**, not in ore. Legitimate **company finance**, **not** world-export RVI.
- **Speculative investment:** “season will pump E.” **Not RVI.**

**Legitimate productive role?** Only **after** a **non-player buyer of output** exists — or as **ordinary game** “patron funds a war” (still players/sponsors). **No financial instruments designed.** The gap is **demand**, not a missing bond primitive.

## 10. Spectators / audience

Watching conflicts, world Events, prestige, entertainment: **real** if Dynasty is **worth watching**. That is **unproven** until play is.

**Demand type:** tickets, subs, clips, **sponsorship on the audience** — **C/D**, sometimes **B**.

**Explicitly not designed: gambling.** Do not “bet on raids” as RVI. Distinguish:

| Entertainment / sponsorship | Betting / speculation |
| --- | --- |
| Pay to **see** or **associate** | Pay because **odds / next trader** |
| No-resale still works | Often **fails** no-resale / next-buyer |

Spectacle **can** feed P20. It **does not** make lots into commodities.

## 11. Collective / social value

Persistent history, famous players, legendary Events, rare discoveries, stories, cultural artifacts.

Outsiders might pay to **attend**, **commission retellings**, **own a keepsake**, **be associated**.

| Kind | Honest name |
| --- | --- |
| Being there / watching | Experiential |
| “I hold the relic” for status | Social/collectible |
| “It will be worth more” | Speculation |

All can exist. **Do not conflate.** Wipe **erases W**; history that **survives** is **P/Identity/media**, not the mill.

This is **not** a substitute for P7 (world activity) or P17 (non-recirculation). A museum of a dead season is **downstream of play**, not a reason the **factory** had external buyers.

## 19. The critical question

### If Dynasty launched tomorrow with no token, no NFTs, and no speculative market, what would an external person rationally pay Dynasty for?

**Direct answer:**

1. **To play** (or to play a premium/event slice) — the game.  
2. **To put a brand on spectacle** — if and only if people already watch or play in numbers worth buying. At launch, this is **hope**, not a contract.  
3. **Optionally:** cosmetics / souvenirs that they **keep for themselves**.  
4. **Not:** ore, mills, Intel dumps, yield, ownership of the map, or “exposure to the economy.”

A wealthy person who **pays to play at scale** is still **(1)**, not a new category.

### Is that enough to support the original crypto-economic thesis?

**No.**

The original thesis (P1, P6, P7, P17, `AUDIT.md`): play in which **building, discovery, ownership, participation, and earnings** can have **real economic significance**, with **external value in that is not player recirculation**, token **not** the economy.

What launch-without-speculation supports:

- A **coherent game economy** (`economic-engine.md`).  
- **Ordinary game revenue** (tickets, maybe cosmetics).  
- **Sponsorship** as a **possible later** media/ad layer — **not demonstrated**, **not** W-export.

What it does **not** support:

- Outsiders paying **because World production is useful to them**.  
- Earnings from **W** that are **not** another player’s money or the operator’s ticket cut.  
- A reason for **settlement-grade S** beyond **holding the ticket pot / sponsor pot / player deposits**.

**P17 can be formally “met”** by ticket+sponsor inflows. That reading **lowers** the thesis to **“games and ads.”** It **abandons** the harder claim that **the world produces something an outsider wants**.

### Smallest genuinely missing economic capability?

**Not** a token.  
**Not** a sixth kernel primitive.  
**Not** an ExternalDemand **domain** until there is something to domain-model.

**Missing is a product** (a **reason**): a specified **non-player use** of something Dynasty produces — **or** an explicit **constitutional decision** that RVI **is** participation+sponsorship and the crypto layer is **only** optional custody/settlement of **those** flows plus player E.

Until one of those two is chosen, the **capability gap** is:

> **A customer who wants Dynasty’s output without needing to be (or to exit to) a player in the W/P/E loop.**

No such customer is identified. **Do not invent one.**

## Close

### A. Definition of external demand

Payment for **use, experience, or association wanted for itself**, by a payer who is **not** merely circulating inside the player W/P/E loop. **Entering with money ≠ external demand.**

### B. External participant categories

Players (including whales and newcomers) = **internal**. Spectators = **audience**. Sponsors = **candidate C**. Capital providers = **finance, not demand**. Collectors = **D/E**. Researchers/service buyers = **usually G or freelance**. Protocol participants = **not customers**.

### C. What Dynasty can plausibly sell

**Play.** Maybe **attention** (sponsorship). Maybe **status cosmetics**. **Not** W commodities. **Not** fog. In-world goods/services/Intel/rights: **to players**.

### D. Paying to participate

Honest **game monetization** and real operator inflow. **Does not** prove the world-export thesis. Do not sneer; do not inflate.

### E. Sponsorship

Best **non-player** candidate: they buy **attention/association/Events**. Needs audience. Distinct from production. Distortion and brand-vs-raid (**O32**) unresolved. P20 still **undesigned**.

### F. Digital goods/rights

Utility **in W** for players. Collectibles **maybe D**. Investment wrappers **E**. Wipe fights perpetual W trophies.

### G. Information

Internal E: strong. External product: **not found**. High risk of unpaid data work and dead fog.

### H. Player-service

On-world: essential E. Off-world: **freelance platform** — reject as thesis.

### I. Real-world utility

**Does not emerge** from current play. Forcing it **redesigns the game**.

### J. Capital providers

No legitimate **production finance** until a **buyer of output** exists. Otherwise speculation or **studio** investment.

### K. Spectator

Entertainment/sponsorship only. **No gambling design.** Downstream of play.

### L. Social/experiential/collectible

Real as **media/status**, not as **ore demand**. Do not conflate with speculation.

### M. No-resale

Play and sponsorship **survive**. Flip assets **die**. That is the split.

### N. No-token

Play, E, sponsorship **survive**. Token-shaped demand **dies**.

### O. No-player

Almost everything **dies**. There is **no** player-independent product.

### P. Circular-demand

Named and **rejected as RVI**: next-buyer, wash, chart, deposit-as-yield.

### Q. Game-integrity

Do not sell observability, mastery, or the only remainder. Scale is already the whale problem.

### R. Classification

See §18. Strong: **B**. Conditional: **C**, **D**. Legitimate but not RVI: **F**. Thesis-killers if used as RVI: **E**. Export: **G**.

### S. Why would an outsider pay?

**To play. Maybe to sponsor an audience. Maybe for a keepsake.** Not for the world as a factory.

### T. Crypto-economic thesis?

**Not supported yet.** Game economy **yes**. Export economy **no**. Ticket+ad **yes as a business**, **no as the original harder claim** unless the corpus **explicitly demotes** P17 to that.

### U. Smallest missing capability
